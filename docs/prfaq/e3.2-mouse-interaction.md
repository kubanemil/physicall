## Press Release

### Mouse Interaction for Particle Simulation

**For** users exploring physical simulations in the browser
**Who** want to interact directly with particles rather than just observe
**The** Mouse Interaction feature
**Is a** new interactive capability
**That** lets users grab, drag, spawn, and delete particles using intuitive mouse controls
**Unlike** the current view-only experience where users can only watch simulations unfold
**Our solution** provides direct manipulation of the simulation, making physics tangible and explorable

---

Physicall now lets you reach into your simulations and interact with particles directly. Click and drag any particle to fling it across the screen, watching it interact with other particles and forces in real-time. Right-click to spawn new particles at your cursor position, or select and delete particles you no longer want.

This transforms Physicall from a passive viewing experience into an interactive physics playground. Want to see what happens when you throw a particle into a cluster? Grab it and find out. Curious how the simulation behaves with more particles in a specific area? Spawn them exactly where you want. The simulation responds instantly to your input, making it an intuitive tool for exploring physics concepts.

## FAQ

### Customer Experience

**Q: How do I drag a particle?**
A: Click and hold on any particle, then move your mouse. The particle follows your cursor, and when you release, it continues with the velocity you gave it. A visual indicator shows which particle you're grabbing.

**Q: How do I spawn new particles?**
A: NEEDS DECISION - Options:
1. Right-click anywhere to spawn a particle at that position
2. Keyboard shortcut (e.g., Shift+Click) to spawn
3. Dedicated "spawn mode" toggle button

**Q: How do I delete particles?**
A: NEEDS DECISION - Options:
1. Right-click on a particle to delete it
2. Keyboard shortcut (e.g., Ctrl+Click or Delete key after selecting)
3. "Delete mode" where clicking removes particles

**Q: Can I select multiple particles?**
A: Not in V1. This is a single-particle interaction feature. Multi-select may come in a future iteration.

**Q: What happens if I drag a particle outside the simulation boundaries?**
A: The particle remains constrained within the simulation bounds. If you drag near an edge, the particle stops at the boundary.

**Q: How do I know which particle I'm about to grab?**
A: NEEDS DECISION - Hover feedback options:
1. Particle highlights/changes color on hover
2. Cursor changes to a grab icon when over a particle
3. Both of the above

### Scope & Non-Goals

**Q: What is explicitly NOT included in V1?**
A:
- Multi-particle selection and group operations
- Touch/mobile gesture support (project non-goal)
- Particle property editing (changing mass, size, etc. via mouse)
- Drawing/painting multiple particles at once
- Undo/redo for particle operations

**Q: What's the minimal version that still delivers the core benefit?**
A: Drag-to-interact only (E3.2.1). Spawning and deleting are secondary - the core value is being able to physically interact with particles and see real-time response.

**Q: What would make us cut scope further?**
A: If ray picking proves complex, we could:
- Start with 2D-only picking (ignore depth)
- Limit to nearest particle within a radius rather than precise picking

**Q: What's deferred to future iterations?**
A:
- Multi-select and group operations
- Particle property editing via UI
- Gesture-based spawning (draw shapes, spray particles)
- Force brushes (apply forces to nearby particles without grabbing)

### Technical Constraint FAQs

**Q: What existing capabilities does this depend on?**
A:
- Camera system (E1.1) - need view/projection matrices for ray casting
- Particle rendering (E2.2) - particles must exist to interact with
- GPU buffer containing particle positions - need to read back for picking

**Q: What new capabilities need to be built?**
A:
- Ray casting from screen coordinates to world space
- Particle picking (find which particle the ray hits)
- Force application to individual particles
- GPU buffer read-back or CPU-side position tracking for picking
- Mouse event handling in TypeScript, passed to WASM

**Q: Are there performance concerns?**
A: NEEDS RESEARCH
- Reading particle positions back from GPU for picking could be slow
- Alternative: maintain CPU-side copy of positions for picking only
- With 10,000+ particles, naive distance checking is O(n) per click

**Q: What's the simplest implementation?**
A:
1. Keep CPU-side copy of particle positions (updated each frame or on-demand)
2. On mouse down, cast ray and find nearest particle within threshold
3. While dragging, apply force toward cursor position in compute shader
4. On release, stop applying force

### Edge Cases & Error States

**Q: What happens if I click where there's no particle?**
A: Nothing happens. The click is ignored for drag purposes. For spawn (if right-click spawns), a new particle appears at that position.

**Q: What happens if particles are moving fast and I try to grab one?**
A: The system picks the particle closest to click position at the moment of click. If particles are moving very fast, this might feel imprecise. Consider adding a small "grab radius" tolerance.

**Q: What if I drag a particle into another particle?**
A: Collision detection (if implemented, E4.1) handles this. If not yet implemented, particles overlap temporarily.

**Q: What if the simulation is paused?**
A: NEEDS DECISION
1. Dragging still works, moving particles to new positions (position-based)
2. Dragging is disabled when paused
3. Dragging works but particle stays where placed (no velocity imparted)

**Q: What happens when spawning exceeds max particle count?**
A: NEEDS DECISION - Either:
1. Silently fail (no spawn)
2. Show brief visual feedback that spawn failed
3. Replace oldest particle (probably not - unexpected behavior)

---

### Business & Operational FAQs

**Q: How will we know if this is successful?**
A:
- User can grab any visible particle and drag it smoothly (no perceivable lag)
- Dragged particle influences/is influenced by simulation forces
- Particle count can be increased/decreased via spawn/delete

**Q: What could go wrong post-launch?**
A:
- Picking feels imprecise with many overlapping particles
- Performance degrades when reading GPU buffers frequently
- Dragging feels "floaty" or unresponsive if force application is wrong

**Q: Are there security considerations?**
A: Minimal - all operations are local to the browser tab. No external communication.

## Implementation Scope

### Current State → End State

| Current State | End State |
|---------------|-----------|
| Users can only observe particles moving | Users can grab and drag any particle |
| Particle count is fixed at simulation start | Users can spawn new particles at cursor position |
| Cannot remove unwanted particles | Users can delete specific particles |
| Camera controls are the only mouse interaction | Mouse has dual purpose: camera (drag background) and particle (drag particle) |

### User Stories

- [ ] As a user, I can click and drag a particle to move it and impart velocity
- [ ] As a user, I can see visual feedback when hovering over a draggable particle
- [ ] As a user, I can spawn a new particle at my cursor position
- [ ] As a user, I can delete a specific particle I no longer want
- [ ] As a user, camera controls still work when not interacting with particles

### Success Criteria

- [ ] Clicking on a particle selects/grabs it with visual feedback
- [ ] Dragging a grabbed particle moves it smoothly with <16ms latency
- [ ] Releasing a dragged particle imparts momentum based on drag velocity
- [ ] Spawning adds a particle at cursor position with default properties
- [ ] Deleting removes a particle from simulation immediately
- [ ] 10,000 particles: picking still responds in <50ms
- [ ] Mouse interaction works correctly at different zoom levels

### Known Compromises

- [ ] Single particle interaction only (no multi-select) - keeps scope manageable for learning project
- [ ] CPU-side position tracking for picking - simpler than GPU readback, slight memory overhead
- [ ] Fixed spawn properties - new particles have default mass/velocity rather than configurable

### Open Questions / Needs Research

- [ ] Best approach for ray-particle intersection with GPU data (CPU copy vs GPU readback vs compute-based picking)
- [ ] How to distinguish camera drag from particle drag (modifier key? click target detection?)
- [ ] Optimal force magnitude for dragging to feel responsive but physical
- [ ] Whether to use velocity-based or position-based dragging
